How items are classified

Priority

Critical
Safety, fire or structural risk, or something that blocks operation or commissioning. Example: no fire protection on a diesel tank, main rebar cut while coring.
High
Major breach of the contract, specification or code, a Non-Rectifiable item, or a repeated one. Example: unsuitable backfill, missing approval before energizing.
Medium
Deviation from drawings or procedures that can be fixed without schedule impact. Example: oil leakage at a flange, cable pulled without an approved route.
Low
Cosmetic or documentation only. Example: cable dressing, missing labels, chamfer defects.

Root cause

Workmanship
Poor execution on site.
Design
Design or specification gap, or deviation from the approved design.
Material
Wrong, unapproved or defective material.
Documentation / Procedure
Missing approval, IR, ECC or method statement, or a procedure breach.
Supervision / QA
Repeated or unsupervised work, a QA failure.
Other
Operational or unclear cause, or the description points to an attachment.

Status and timing

Active
Open, Reject or Under Approval.
Reject
The client rejected the contractor’s reply, so the item stays active.
Overdue
Past the log’s target date, or without one, older than the limit set above.
Escalate
Active for longer than the escalate limit set above.

Phase (NCR only)

ICOD
Simple Cycle phase (Initial COD).
PCOD
Combined Cycle phase (Plant COD).

Active means Open, Reject or Under Approval. Reject means the client rejected the reply, so the item stays active. Priority and root cause were assigned by Claude reading each subject and description; they are not in your log, so review them in the Excel file.
